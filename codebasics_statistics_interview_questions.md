# Statistics Interview Questions

Collected and organized from Codebasics math & stats materials.

## Beginner

1. Imagine you're conducting a survey to understand people's opinion about a new product, but you only collect responses from people in a shopping mall. What type of bias might this introduce and why?
2. What does the Law of Large Numbers state in statistics, and why is it important?
3. What is the difference between a parameter and a statistic in statistics?
4. What is the difference between a normal distribution and a standard normal distribution? Why do we convert data to a standard normal distribution?
5. What is the Standard Normal Distribution, and how is data typically distributed within ±1, ±2, and ±3 standard deviations from the mean?
6. What is a hypothesis in statistics? Explain the difference between a null hypothesis and an alternative hypothesis with a real-life example.
7. What is a One-Tailed and Two-Tailed Test in Statistics? Give examples for each.
8. What is Alpha (α) in Hypothesis Testing?
9. In what situation would the median be a more suitable measure compared to the mean?
10. What are the characteristics of a bell-curve distribution?
11. What are Degrees of Freedom in Statistics?

## Intermediate

1. You survey 20 people about their daily screen time and get an average of 5.4 hours. Your colleague surveys 2,000 people from the same population and gets 5.0 hours. Which concept explains why the larger survey's average is likely closer to the true population average?
2. What is the Central Limit Theorem (CLT)? Why is the CLT important in statistics?
3. What happens to the mean, variance, and standard deviation when comparing a population distribution with the sampling distribution of the sample mean?
4. What is the Standard Error (SE) in Statistics?
5. You're analyzing the income data of individuals in a city. The distribution is heavily right-skewed because a few people earn significantly higher than the rest. You've been asked to identify and remove outliers, but your teammate says "Let's use mean ± 3 standard deviations". As a Data Scientist, how would you respond? What method would you use instead to handle outliers in this skewed dataset?
6. Explain what a z-score is in statistics. What is its significance?
7. Suppose you take a random sample of 30 students and find their average height is 170 cm with a standard deviation of 10 cm. How would you calculate a 95% confidence interval for the true average height of the population? Also, explain what the result means.
8. What are some common statistical methods used to detect outliers in a dataset, and how do they work?
9. You are testing a new blood pressure medication. Describe how Type I and Type II errors would apply in this context, and discuss the trade-offs involved in setting the significance level (α). How would changing α affect the probability of Type II error (β), and how would you balance the risk in a high-stakes environment like healthcare?
10. You are analyzing customer churn data for a subscription service. The dataset is highly imbalanced, with only 8% of customers churning. You're tasked with building a model to predict churn. What statistical challenges do you anticipate, and how would you address them? Discuss your approach in terms of model evaluation, error types, and any statistical adjustments or techniques you would use.
11. An e-commerce company wants to evaluate two different designs for its checkout page. One version uses a single-step process, while the other features a multi-step design with trust badges and a progress bar. The business wants to know which design leads to a higher purchase completion rate. How would you design an experiment to help make a data-driven decision, and what factors would you consider during analysis?
12. A marketing team runs three different promotional email campaigns (Campaign A, B, and C) to boost customer purchases. After the campaign, they record whether each recipient made a purchase (Yes/No). How would you determine if the type of email campaign has a significant impact on purchase behaviour? Describe your approach, the statistical test you'd use, assumptions involved, and how you'd handle challenges like multiple comparisons or unbalanced groups.
13. Why is the t-test not used for a two-sample test of proportions?
14. What are the different types of t-tests, and when should each be used?
15. How can you determine if two samples have equal variance?
16. You are tasked with estimating the average daily screen time of teenagers across the country. How would you approach this problem using statistical methods?
17. You are given two datasets with the same mean and standard deviation. What additional descriptive measures would you use to understand the data distribution better, and why?
18. How does sample size affect the reliability of descriptive statistics like standard deviation and variance?
